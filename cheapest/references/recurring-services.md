# Monthly storage and VPS offers

Use the shared discovery, access and comparison workflow. These are additional
comparison details, not fixed questionnaires or mandatory provider lists.

## Luggage storage

Distinguish a staffed hourly/daily bag drop from monthly self-storage or valet
storage. Start with bag count and approximate dimensions, neighborhood, duration
and retrieval needs. If duration is open, show first-month cost and ongoing rate;
do not force the user to choose a long contract just to begin searching.

Search the neighborhood for actual facilities and by-item pickup services, then
inspect each provider's own inventory and offers. In New York, examples include
Manhattan Mini Storage, Storage Post, CubeSmart and urBin; availability varies.
Daily services such as Bounce, Stasher or LuggageHero are a separate route and
may require a long-stay quote. A service-area page does not mean its warehouse
is in that neighborhood or that customers can walk in to retrieve bags.

Match usable dimensions including height/door opening, unit level, stairs or
lifting, access hours, climate control and retrieval charges. Tiny top lockers
can cost less than floor-level units; verify suitability rather than selecting
the cheapest headline. Do not require identical unit dimensions if both fit.

Read the selected offer's move-in calculation: prorated rent, admin fee, lock,
required coverage/protection, tax and payment fees. Check whether an existing
lock or proof of other coverage changes cost. Compare the planned duration with
minimum stay/prepayment, promo months, post-promo rent, coverage after any free
period, move-out notice and refunds. A “first month free” offer may require three
months paid upfront. Do not subtract a transport voucher from storage rent or
stack it with a rental discount without verified terms. If a quote requires
contact details or reserving a unit, retain the public lead and missing costs;
do not submit a sales inquiry or hold during research.

## VPS and other hosting subscriptions

Start with the workload: always-on agent/API client, coding/build tasks, or local
model inference; these need different resources. Set a modest initial RAM/CPU/
disk estimate and identify useful lower-cost or higher-capacity alternatives.
Location near New York is a latency preference unless stated as a hard rule.
Check architecture, supported OS, root access and needed runtime compatibility;
a shared web-hosting plan is not a VPS. Agent/model subscriptions and API usage
are separate costs from hosting. Do not assume a small VPS can run a local LLM
or many simultaneous browser/build jobs because it advertises AI support.

Discover direct providers and current specials. For example compare OVHcloud,
RackNerd, Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Vultr, netcup, IONOS and regional alternatives
that fit; no provider is a bundled integration. Follow promising prices to the
actual configuration page. Verify exact plan generation, selectable region,
stock, currency/tax region, billed term, setup fee, IPv4, storage, transfer and
backup charges. A provider's global location list does not prove a special is
available there. Public control-panel demos do not establish real deployment.
Resolve region labels to the actual city when it matters; “New York” can mean
Buffalo rather than New York City. Do not infer latency without measuring it.

Compare cash due today, commitment, total over the same period, post-promo and
renewal rates, cancellation, and refund terms. Trial credit is a temporary
scenario, not the continuing monthly cost. Record shared versus dedicated CPU
and material fair-use limits when the workload makes them relevant. Do not
equate core counts with measured speed or vendor uptime claims with observed
reliability. Recommend on verified price/features and state when performance
was not tested. Stop before checkout submission or provisioning.
